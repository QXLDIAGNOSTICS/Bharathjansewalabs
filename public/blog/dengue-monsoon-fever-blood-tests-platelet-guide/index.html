Why Timely Testing Matters in Dengue:

Dengue is transmitted by the daytime-biting Aedes aegypti mosquito. In urban Bengaluru, rapid weather shifts often lead to localized outbreaks. Identifying the infection during the first 48 hours allows physicians to establish hydration protocols before the critical phase (days 4 to 7) begins.

1. Dengue NS1 Antigen (Days 1–4):

The non-structural protein 1 (NS1) is secreted into the blood during active viral replication. It becomes detectable within hours of fever onset, making it the primary marker for early diagnosis.

2. Dengue IgM / IgG Antibodies (Day 5 Onwards):

As the body mounts an immune response, IgM antibodies emerge around day 5 and persist for several weeks. IgG antibodies indicate previous infection or convalescent immunity.

3. Why Daily CBC Platelet Tracking Is Critical:

The primary medical risk in severe dengue is capillary leakage and thrombocytopenia (rapid drop in platelets). While normal platelet counts range between 150,000 and 450,000 / μL, counts dropping below 100,000 require close physician monitoring. Simultaneously, rising Hematocrit (PCV) signals plasma leakage that necessitates urgent clinical intervention.
