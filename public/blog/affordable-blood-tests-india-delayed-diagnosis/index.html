When a Blood Test Becomes Too Expensive: Why Affordable Diagnostics Matter for Every Indian Family

👨‍⚕️ Dr Shantakumar Muruda, MD (Biochemistry), Consultant Biochemistry & Lead Assessor, NABL 📅 18 November 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✓ Verified Clinical Article
Affordable healthcare begins with affordable diagnosis.

For millions of Indian families, the cost of healthcare is an important factor in deciding when to seek medical attention. When laboratory tests are difficult to access or expensive, people may postpone investigations recommended by their doctors. This can delay the information needed to diagnose illness, monitor treatment and make timely healthcare decisions.

Bharath Jan Sewa Labs is working toward a simple goal: making essential blood tests and routine diagnostic services more affordable and accessible to families across Bengaluru.

The Hidden Healthcare Problem: “We Will Get the Test Done Next Month”

Imagine a family living on a modest monthly income.

A parent has been advised to undergo a blood sugar test. A grandparent requires regular kidney-function monitoring. Someone else has been prescribed a complete blood count because of persistent tiredness.

Every test has a medical purpose. But the family also has rent, groceries, transport, school fees and other essential expenses.

The tests are postponed—not necessarily because the family considers them unimportant, but because healthcare must compete with immediate financial needs.

Clinical Reality: This is an illustrative situation, not an individual patient case. However, research in India confirms that financial constraints, travel expenses, service availability and long waiting times can affect healthcare-seeking behaviour.

A 2025 systematic review of healthcare utilisation among people with noncommunicable diseases in India identified financial and practical barriers that can lead people to delay or avoid care.

A postponed test is not always a harmless postponement. Depending on the condition, delays can mean that clinicians receive important diagnostic information later than needed.

India's Healthcare Costs: What the Latest National Data Reveals

India has made considerable progress in expanding public healthcare financing. Yet households continue to carry a substantial share of healthcare expenditure.

📊 National Health Accounts (NHA) Estimates 2023–24 (Released November 2026):

  • Household out-of-pocket expenditure (OOPE) accounted for 42.7% of India's total health expenditure in 2023–24.
  • This was lower than 64.2% in 2013–14, indicating meaningful progress in financial protection and public insurance.
  • Nevertheless, direct household spending remains a major part of India's healthcare financing.

*These figures represent spending on healthcare overall, not the percentage spent specifically on diagnostic tests. Source: Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, National Health Accounts 2023–24.

Diagnostic affordability matters particularly for people who need repeated monitoring, such as patients managing diabetes or chronic kidney conditions. A single test may be affordable, but recurring tests, consultation fees, medicines and travel can add up.

How Delayed Diagnostic Testing Can Affect Health

Many medical conditions cannot be assessed reliably from symptoms alone. Laboratory investigations help healthcare professionals identify abnormalities, evaluate risk and monitor treatment.

1. Diabetes and Blood Glucose Monitoring

Diabetes may develop with few noticeable symptoms. Blood glucose and HbA1c tests can support diagnosis or ongoing monitoring when appropriately prescribed. If recommended testing is repeatedly postponed, clinicians may have less timely information to assess glucose control or adjust care.

2. Kidney Health

Tests such as serum creatinine, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and urine albumin assessment can provide important information about kidney health. Some forms of kidney disease may progress without obvious early symptoms. Clinically appropriate monitoring is therefore important for people at increased risk.

3. Anaemia and Persistent Fatigue

A complete blood count can help detect abnormalities such as low haemoglobin. When indicated, further investigations may help identify iron deficiency or other causes of anaemia. Persistent fatigue should not automatically be attributed to anaemia, and the correct tests depend on the patient's symptoms and clinical history.

4. Thyroid Disorders

TSH and other thyroid-function tests may be used when a healthcare professional suspects thyroid dysfunction or needs to monitor an existing condition. Getting appropriate testing at the recommended time can support more informed treatment decisions.

💡 The message is not that everyone needs frequent blood tests. It is that medically necessary testing should not be delayed simply because it is financially difficult to obtain.

Essential Diagnostics Are a Public Health Priority in India

The Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) published its updated National Essential Diagnostics List (NEDL) in 2025.

The list identifies important diagnostic investigations recommended at different healthcare facility levels. It highlights the importance of making appropriate testing available throughout the health system.

The World Health Organization has also examined how sample transport networks and access to diagnostics can help strengthen primary healthcare services in Indian states (WHO: Implementation of Diagnostics Services for Primary Health Care in Four Indian States).

These efforts reinforce an important principle: access to diagnostic testing should not depend solely on whether a patient lives near a large hospital or can afford expensive private services.

*NEDL is a national diagnostic planning resource, not a commercial laboratory certification. Individual laboratories must separately meet the applicable quality and accreditation requirements.

Does a Lower Test Price Mean Lower Laboratory Quality?

Not necessarily. Price alone is not a reliable measure of diagnostic quality.

Laboratory quality depends on appropriate methods, validated equipment, trained personnel, internal quality control, external quality assessment, sample handling, transportation conditions and competent medical reporting.

Patients can ask any collection centre, including BJSL, for the name of the laboratory that will perform their test.

Affordability and quality must work together. The objective is not simply to make a test inexpensive, but to ensure that patients receive clinically useful and reliable results at a transparent cost.

What If Someone Cannot Afford Even a Low-Cost Test?

For some families, even a small diagnostic expense may be difficult.

Patients who cannot afford medically recommended testing should ask their treating healthcare professional about available government hospital laboratory services, Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, public diagnostic programmes and other applicable assistance options.

Affordable private laboratory networks can complement—not replace—these public services by providing additional neighbourhood testing choices.

They can. When an investigation is clinically necessary, postponing it may delay information needed for diagnosis, monitoring or treatment decisions. However, not every medical condition requires immediate testing, and the appropriate timing should be determined by a healthcare professional.
